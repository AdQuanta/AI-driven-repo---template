# Night-Time Pressure Reduction and Burst Frequency

## Abstract
> Water mains burst more often when they run at high pressure, and network pressure peaks at night, when demand is lowest. We report a two-year trial on a fictional utility in which pressure-reducing valves lowered night-time pressure by 20% in 12 districts. Burst frequency in those districts fell by 28% relative to the two years before the trial. Because every burst releases water until it is isolated, the fall in bursts also reduced the water lost to bursts. We describe the valve settings, the burst records, and the comparison with untreated districts.

## Key Takeaways
- **Core Contribution:** A two-year field trial of time-based night-time pressure reduction.
- **Methodology:** Pressure-reducing valves in 12 districts lowered outlet pressure by 20% between midnight and five in the morning; bursts were counted from repair records and compared with 12 untreated districts.
- **Results:** Cutting night-time pressure by 20% reduced burst frequency by 28% across 12 districts over two years (Section 3.1). The estimated water lost to bursts fell by 24% (Section 3.2).
- **Does not cover:** leak localization, metering (Section 4).
