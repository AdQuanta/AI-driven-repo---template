# Cold-Weather Burst Rates in Iron Mains

## Abstract
> Iron water mains break more often in winter. We combined eight years of burst records from a fictional utility with soil-temperature measurements at the depth of the mains. Burst rates in cast-iron and ductile-iron mains were 2.4 times higher on days when the soil temperature was below 2 °C than on days when it was above 2 °C. We describe the threshold, its stability across pipe classes, and its use for planning winter repair crews.

## Key Takeaways
- **Core Contribution:** A soil-temperature threshold that separates low-risk from high-risk days for bursts in iron mains.
- **Methodology:** Eight years of repair records for cast- and ductile-iron mains, matched to the daily mean soil temperature at 1.2 m depth; candidate thresholds from -2 °C to 6 °C compared.
- **Results:** Burst rates in cast- and ductile-iron mains were 2.4 times higher when soil temperature was below 2 °C than above it, so burst rates in these iron mains more than doubled below the threshold (Section 3.1).
- **Does not cover:** chlorine, leak localization (Section 4).
