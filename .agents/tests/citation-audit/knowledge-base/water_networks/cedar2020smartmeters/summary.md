# Inlet Smart Meters for Night-Flow Anomaly Detection

## Abstract
> A new leak in a district adds a steady flow that is easiest to see at night, when legitimate demand is lowest. We installed smart meters at the inlets of ten districts of a fictional network and opened a hydrant in each to simulate a new leak. A simple night-flow anomaly rule flagged the simulated leak within 24 hours in 9 of the 10 trials. Detecting a new leak within a day, rather than at the next scheduled survey, shortens the time it runs before repair and so reduces the water it loses.

## Key Takeaways
- **Core Contribution:** A test of district-inlet smart meters as an early alarm for new leaks.
- **Methodology:** Inlet meters logged flow every 15 minutes; a rule flagged nights whose minimum night flow exceeded the 30-day rolling median by more than three median absolute deviations; hydrants were opened unannounced to simulate leaks.
- **Results:** Smart meters at district inlets flagged night-flow anomalies from new leaks within 24 hours in 9 of 10 trials (Section 3.1). Early detection shortens a leak's run time and the water it loses (Section 3.3).
- **Does not cover:** pressure control, bursts (Section 4).
