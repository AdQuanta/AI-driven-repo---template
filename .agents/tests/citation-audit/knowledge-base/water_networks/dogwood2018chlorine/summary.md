# Chlorine Decay in Municipal Storage Tanks

## Abstract
> Treated water loses its free chlorine while it sits in storage tanks, and a tank that holds water too long can deliver water below the residual a utility must maintain. We sampled eight storage tanks of a fictional municipal system weekly for a year. Free chlorine decayed at 0.08 to 0.15 mg/L per day, and the decay was faster in the warm months than in the cool months. We relate the decay rate to water age and give a simple rule for the maximum residence time that keeps the residual above its target.

## Key Takeaways
- **Core Contribution:** A year of weekly measurements of free-chlorine decay in eight storage tanks.
- **Methodology:** Inlet and outlet free chlorine measured weekly; decay rate is the inlet-to-outlet drop divided by the mean residence time.
- **Results:** Free chlorine in storage tanks decays at 0.08 to 0.15 mg/L per day (Section 3.1), faster in warm months (Section 3.2).
- **Does not cover:** pipe bursts, temperature effects on pipes, cold weather (Section 4).
